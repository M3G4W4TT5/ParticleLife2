# Visual tools and capture

Appearance controls change the view without changing particle motion, interaction ranges, numerical timestep or population. Presets and viewer companions retain these settings. Older files use the original species colours, normal blending and no trails or glow.

**Light and motion** provides opacity, normal/additive blending, speed brightness, speed colour and direction colour. Speed brightness runs from 20% at rest to full brightness at the reference speed. Speed colour runs from blue through green to red at that speed; direction uses a hue wheel. CPU rendering converts velocities for display only, preserving direction at extreme magnitudes. These converted values never enter a simulation or scientific save.

Trails combine completed particle images. Their half-life uses presentation time, freezes while paused and resumes without counting the paused interval. Ordinary position edits while paused retain the prior image as trail history. New experiment, Reset, camera, palette and other display changes clear old trails. Toggle trails off and on to see only the current geometry. Glow uses a bounded blurred particle layer and nine additive samples; it does not blur the controls or world grid. Both features consume extra render targets and overdraw. Set their strength/half-life to zero to disable them.

Enabled trails retain two RGBA32F history images and apply a floating-point decay factor to premultiplied colour and alpha. This avoids permanent dim residue from repeated 8-bit rounding and preserves long half-lives at high frame rates. The history pair costs 32 bytes per full scene render-target pixel, including the area behind panels rather than only the visible world: about 66.4 MB for a 1920 × 1080 scene, excluding driver overhead and the separate particle/glow targets. Disabling trails releases both histories; glow alone does not allocate them. If history allocation or the required render-target format is unavailable, a status message explains why trails are disabled while particles and glow remain usable. Resizing or toggling trails permits a fresh allocation attempt.

**Observations and tracking** is optional. Population heatmaps use a 32 by 32 display grid. Spatial-bin mode follows the maximum interaction cutoff with up to 128 cells per axis. While tracking is active, both visible overlay modes share the tracking sample at 128 by 128 cells. The next sample after tracking stops returns to the selected mode's normal resolution. These are observation bins, separate from the numerical solver's neighbour grid; particles outside the display rectangle are counted separately and remain in the simulation. The sample's completed step/time, time since receipt and sampling cost are displayed. The interval controls observation work, not simulation speed.

Pick a circular region on the canvas to track the population-weighted centres of its occupied bins. This is a coarse visual heuristic: it does not identify an organism or promise to keep the same particle identities. Empty regions retain their last centre and show lost contact. Periodic tracking uses minimum-image displacement across seams. Pan or zoom suspends camera follow while retaining the indicator; **Recenter on region** resumes follow. Tracking is transient; saving records the configured camera target rather than a sequence of automatic camera movements.

**Screenshots and animation** saves the canvas without control panels. PNG retains full colour. GIF uses a fixed 256-colour palette and a simple bounded encoder, so files can be larger than videos or highly compressed GIFs. Choose width, frame rate and duration before recording. GIFs are limited to ten seconds and 80 million requested frame pixels; reduce those controls if a request exceeds the limit. Resizing during recording letterboxes the canvas into the fixed output size.

Pixel readback occurs only during an explicit capture. PNG encoding runs with that capture and reports its wall cost. GIF encoding runs on a bounded background writer; a busy writer skips images while the saved frame delays retain elapsed presentation time. The UI reports encoded/skipped frames and peak readback/copy cost. Capture overhead can reduce achieved playback speed, but never changes the fixed numerical timestep. Existing media paths are protected, and a GIF is published only after its writer completes successfully.

GIF delays use cumulative elapsed presentation time rounded to hundredths of a second, preventing frame-by-frame rounding from shortening a recording. Timing starts at the first accepted image; each encoded frame still has GIF's minimum one-hundredth-second delay. The fixed RGB 3-3-2 palette can visibly shift subtle greys and dark colours. Use PNG when exact canvas colours matter.
